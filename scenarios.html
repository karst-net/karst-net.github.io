field notes

Nine moments people actually asked for.

These are the situations beta testers describe when we ask why they wanted Karst. Most are things it already handles directly; two turned out to need a recipe built from existing pieces rather than a built-in feature — and we'd rather show our work than let either sound simpler than it is.

SC-01

Coffee shop Wi-Fi becomes a non-issue.

The moment Karst is up, the network you're sitting on stops mattering. Every connection is encrypted and authenticated before it ever reaches the shop's router.

ZERO TRUST · PHREATIC HANDSHAKE

SC-02

Bring your home network with you.

Route your travel connection through a device you already trust back home, so the far side sees your own network instead of a hotel's. Whether the destination is fine with that is between you and it.

CONSENTED EXIT NODE

SC-03

Print the form. Just the form.

Reach one printer on your home network from the road — and nothing else on it. The route goes exactly where you point it, not the whole house.

SUBNET ROUTE · SCOPED ACL

SC-04

Grab the file, not the network.

Reach a restricted file share while you're away, limited to the same people who were always allowed near it. Karst's default-deny policy and the share's own login are separate, additive checks.

SUBNET ROUTE · GROUP POLICY

SC-05

See where a device you administer goes.

Run the exit node yourself for a computer you hold admin rights on, and its traffic becomes visible at your own gateway — no hidden reporting, nothing new to build. It only works for a device you administer; Karst won't force this onto one you don't.

EXIT NODE · LOCAL ADMIN CONSENT

SC-06

Filter the web on your own schedule.

Point a device's DNS at a filtering resolver you choose, and get category blocking plus time-of-day rules from it directly — no new Karst feature involved. It's DNS-based, so it can be routed around by a browser with its own built-in resolver, the same limit any network-level filtering has.

GROUP-SCOPED DNS UPSTREAM

SC-07

Show one teammate what's on localhost.

Point a single coworker at your work-in-progress server for a quick look — no public tunnel, no exposed port, nothing standing between the two of you but a policy rule you can delete when the review's done.

DIRECT PEER PATH

SC-08

See where your mesh actually lives.

Relay regions and path telemetry tell your infrastructure team where clients really connect from, so relay placement follows your people instead of a guess.

RELAY REGISTRY · TELEMETRY

SC-09

Retire the VPN concentrator.

Move off OpenVPN or AWS VPN on your own schedule: stand up Karst alongside what you have, cut over deliberately, and keep default-deny access control the whole way through.

SELF-HOSTED CONTROL PLANE

!
SC-05 and SC-06 are recipes, not built-in reports.

Both work by pointing a device you administer at infrastructure you already control — an exit node, or a filtering DNS resolver — not by Karst watching or filtering anything itself. Neither one works against a device its own user administers, and Karst still has no general way to schedule non-web access by time of day. We'd rather say so than let the cards above sound bigger than they are.
